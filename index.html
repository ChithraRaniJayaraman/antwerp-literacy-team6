Project Overview

Teenagers in Antwerp are reading less, and often without joy. This project turns that challenge into an actionable, research-grounded improvement path for schools.

What this website includes

  • A clear picture of reading decline in Flemish schools.
  • Three goals grounded in school improvement evidence.
  • Three practical approaches: Self-Regulated Learning, Peer Tutoring, and Parental Involvement.
  • A five-year phased implementation roadmap.
  • Ready-to-use templates for students, teachers, and families.

Core message

This blueprint is not about telling schools what to do. It is about giving schools tools, evidence, and confidence to shape reading improvement in ways that fit local communities.

Reading is not only a language outcome. It is the foundation for learning across mathematics, science, history, and social participation.

The Problem: Do teenagers actually read?

The short answer from current evidence: not much, and not happily.

Why reading became unpopular

  • Screens and short-form media offer instant rewards.
  • Reading in school is often framed as tests and grades, not choice.
  • Low confidence leads to avoidance, especially for second-language learners.
  • Some homes have fewer Dutch-language reading supports.
  • Book choices often fail to reflect students' identities.

Why this matters

  • Reading unlocks performance in all subjects.
  • Strong reading builds vocabulary, empathy, and critical thinking.
  • In multilingual Antwerp, Dutch reading supports school and social integration.
  • This is not only about test scores — it is about opportunity.

Three Interdependent Goals

Lasting change requires teacher capacity, a school-wide reading culture, and student ownership. Missing one weakens the other two.

Goal 1: Build teacher capacity

Teachers need practical strategies, time, and collegial support to integrate reading across subjects.

Goal 2: Build school-wide culture

Reading must become visible and normal across the whole school day, not only in language classes.

Goal 3: Build student ownership

Students choose, set goals, monitor progress, and reflect — shifting reading from duty to agency.

Who does what

  • School leaders: create time, policy alignment, and resources.
  • Teachers: use strategies consistently and monitor engagement.
  • Students: set goals and track growth.
  • Families: reinforce reading habits through simple routines.

Three Practical Approaches

Use the tabs to explore implementation details from the blueprint.

Approach 1: Students take the wheel

Students choose reading materials, set reading goals, monitor their progress, and reflect on learning. The emphasis is ownership: reading as something students do for themselves, not only for compliance.

You cannot force motivation, but you can structure classrooms so motivation can emerge through choice, relevance, and visible progress.

What this looks like in practice

  • Personal reading goals set per term.
  • Reading progress trackers used weekly.
  • Short reflection prompts after each text.
  • Teacher feedback focused on growth and strategy use.

Approach 2: Reading together works

Peer tutoring leverages student-to-student learning: older-younger pairs, same-age pairs, and reciprocal roles. One student reads, the other supports, then both discuss meaning.

Three models that work

  • Cross-age tutoring between year levels.
  • Same-class pair tutoring with rotating roles.
  • Reciprocal peer discussion circles around short texts.

Simple 20-minute session structure

  • 5 min: warm-up and vocabulary preview.
  • 10 min: read aloud + support prompts.
  • 5 min: discussion and one takeaway.

Approach 3: Families as reading partners

Family involvement increases motivation and skill growth. This does not require perfect Dutch, many books at home, or long study routines. It requires connection and accessible school communication.

What this looks like in practice

  • Termly family reading evenings across schools.
  • Multilingual guidance sheets for simple home reading routines.
  • Low-threshold communication channels with carers.
  • Celebrating reading in any language as a pathway to Dutch literacy.

Five-Year Implementation Plan

Start small, scale what works, and institutionalize so the change survives staffing and policy shifts.

Phase 1

Year 1 • Pilot and Build Foundation
  • Select 2-3 pilot schools with varied contexts.
  • Assign a reading coordinator.
  • Train first teacher cohort on 1-2 strategies.
  • Run student reading-interest survey.
  • Launch initial peer tutoring pairs and one family evening.
  • Capture practical learning notes from pilots.

Phase 2

Years 2-3 • Scale and Embed
  • Expand implementation to all schools.
  • Use pilot teachers to train colleagues.
  • Introduce daily 15-20 minute reading routine.
  • Roll out peer tutoring broadly (including cross-school options).
  • Host regular family evenings each term.
  • Track progress via student and teacher tools.

Phase 3

Years 4-5 • Sustain and Institutionalize
  • Embed reading into school improvement plans and budgets.
  • Integrate reading strategies into new staff onboarding.
  • Use experienced teachers as internal trainers.
  • Track equity outcomes and voluntary reading behavior.
  • Celebrate milestones and keep momentum visible.

Conclusion

This blueprint starts from a difficult reality: many Antwerp teenagers read less and read with less pleasure. But it also shows that schools already have what they need to begin: evidence, practical tools, and people capable of building sustainable change.

The goal is not a short-term project. The goal is a way of working that remains strong beyond one school year, one coordinator, or one initiative cycle.
